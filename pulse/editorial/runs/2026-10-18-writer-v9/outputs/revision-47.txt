The Education Department has recommended denying renewal of the American Bar Association’s federal recognition as a law school accreditor, and the final decision now rests with Under Secretary Nicholas Kent, according to Fox Business and Fox News.

The ABA’s accrediting arm has held that federally recognized role since 1952, Fox Business reported. The department said its staff reviewed the ABA’s standards and found the organization “out of compliance” with federal accreditor regulations, Fox Business reported.

The review has already gone through a federal advisory panel. Higher Ed Dive reported that the National Advisory Committee on Institutional Quality and Integrity split 8-8 in two separate votes on how to proceed after spending roughly seven hours deliberating and listening to comments from the public, Education Department staff, and the accreditor. The panel also considered whether to recommend that the law school accreditor be subject to additional oversight for a year, then voted 11-1 on Thursday to report that it could not reach a recommendation. Under Secretary Nicholas Kent has 90 days to make the final call, Higher Ed Dive reported.

Higher Ed Dive reported that the ABA’s accrediting council accredits nearly 200 law schools. It also reported that a council representative said only five of those institutions rely on the agency’s accreditation to access federal student aid. In an earlier report, Higher Ed Dive said the council acts as the gatekeeper for federal aid for just over a dozen freestanding institutions of legal education that are not affiliated with larger colleges or universities.

That distinction matters because losing federal recognition would not automatically strip the ABA of its power to accredit law schools or change state bar-admission rules, Fox News reported. State supreme courts generally set those rules, and many still require graduation from an ABA-accredited law school to sit for the bar exam.

Melissa Hart, chair of the ABA’s Accreditation Council, said in a statement to Fox News Digital that the council is complying with federal law and looks forward to addressing concerns at its hearing before NACIQI. She said, “As a national accrediting body for American law schools, we remain focused on ensuring quality legal education that produces competent, ethical attorneys who are eligible for licensure.”

Fox News also reported that Adam Biggs, general counsel of the University of Austin, said the federal decision is only the first step and could push state supreme courts to consider alternative pathways. He said the school is considering a two-year law program or a combined undergraduate and legal studies model, with free tuition, no federal funding, and more reliance on practicing attorneys and federal judges as adjunct instructors.

The Justice Department has also pressed the ABA over its faculty rules. Fox News reported that the department sent a letter on Oct. 7 pressing the ABA to revise or justify those rules, and The Wall Street Journal reported that the department has threatened an antitrust investigation over restrictions on adjunct professors teaching core law school courses.

Fox News reported that if the ABA loses federal recognition, the most immediate federal-aid consequences would fall on freestanding law schools that rely on the ABA rather than a parent university’s institutional accreditation. Schools tied to larger universities would likely rely on the university’s accreditor for federal student aid, Fox Business reported.

The Education Department’s final decision will determine whether the ABA keeps its federal status, but it would not automatically rewrite state bar rules, though it could spur states to consider alternative pathways.
